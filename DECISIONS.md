- 2026-07-28: **Refined the pre-1906 India timezone handling further: Bombay and Calcutta specifically kept their own official local civil time well past 1906.** Prompted by an optional ask to spot-check the pre-1906 LMT logic against a real published value -- found more than a citation, found a real gap. Implementation (`src/engine/timezone.ts`): added an `INDIA_EXTENDED_LOCAL_TIME` table keyed by GeoNames geonameId (Mumbai 1275339, Kolkata 1275004) so this only fires for births specifically geocoded to those two cities, not all of India -- `geocodePlace()` (`geocoding.ts`) now exposes `geonameId` on its result, threaded through `resolveLocation()` (`location.ts`) into `resolveUtcOffset()`. A Bombay or Calcutta birth between 1906 and their respective cutover now gets the documented fixed offset (taking precedence over both the generic pre-1906 longitude estimate and national IST) instead of silently defaulting to national IST for that whole window, which would have been wrong by 39 minutes (Bombay) or 23 minutes 20 seconds (Calcutta) -- large enough to matter for Ascendant-sensitive claims. Tests added to `tests/timezone.test.ts` and `tests/location.test.ts` covering both cities' extended windows, the switch back to national IST at each cutover, and confirming a third Indian city (Chennai) is unaffected.

- 2026-07-28: **Bug found and fixed while wiring this: a 1ms floating-point rounding error in `julianDayUTToUtcISO()`.** Subtracting two ~2.4-million-magnitude floats (`julianDayUT - JULIAN_DAY_UNIX_EPOCH`) and scaling by 86.4M left ~0.01ms of floating-point noise, which for an exact birth time (e.g. 15:30:00.000) rounded DOWN across a whole-minute boundary (09:59:59.999Z instead of 10:00:00.000Z), silently reconstituting as 15:29 instead of 15:30 one layer up. - 2026-07-29: **P7b design gate: precision target decided BEFORE any search-implementation code, per `BACKLOG.md`'s requirement. Target: nearest UTC calendar day, not finer.** Reasoning: (1) Consistency -- this project already has an established date-level tolerance for boundary timing: dasha boundaries are validated/reported to 1-day granularity (`requirements-spec.md` §10; the Moon-longitude-propagation entries above). (2) Astrological honesty -- Jupiter and Saturn are the two slowest classical grahas, moving roughly 0.08 deg/day (Jupiter, ~11.86y sidereal period) and 0.03 deg/day (Saturn, ~29.46y).
